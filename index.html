About me

I'm an 11th grader at The King's Academy in Sunnyvale, a competitive recurve archer and a certified USA Archery Level 1 Instructor.

I've always been drawn to technology, especially figuring out why something isn't working and fixing it. That curiosity pulled me into programming: I learned Python in UC Berkeley's Introduction to Computer Science program and took AP Computer Science Principles at school.

Archery has taught me patience and what steady practice can do. Since 2024 I've competed in 22 tournaments and climbed to 10th in the California state rankings.

Away from the range and the screen, I grow plants from seed, read world history, collect unique things and volunteer at a Korean language school. Next, I want to get back into guitar and saxophone and keep building my web development skills, starting with this site.

Raising and caring for button quail: what I've learned about their habitat, feeding and day-to-day behavior.

Button Quail Essay

When I first looked at the video of people raising button quail, I was stunned. How could a life so small happen? It was the smallest creature I ever saw. I really wanted one. So I begged my parents for a chance at an experience like the people on youtube of course. They told me to write an essay, and I did. Here I learned even more about their unique size and behavior, of how they tend to live and be, how they are treated, and what illnesses they might have, and most importantly, who raises them. I called them Micro avian birds because people could raise them nearly anywhere like some people raising them in small two room apartments in new york with an aquarium to kids raising them in farms in professional hutches. I was fascinated and I felt like I could do this.

After the essay I ordered my incubator and eggs. Luckily I learned how to use them fast and sought after reddit posts on what to actually do. I put them in, waited 18 days, and I saw my first chicks. My initial batch of quails were 6 out of 12, a solid 50 percent hatch rate, like the reddit posts said about shipped eggs. I put them in the brooder, a plastic 160qt tote with 3 sq ft and some water and food. I used protein feed in pellets the size of ⅛ of a pinky fingernail. I crushed them with some plastic lid I had so they could eat it. But one after another, they started dying. Every morning I found more dead, and after school I did even more. Eventually, all of them died and I was devastated. I felt so sorry and yet so mad about the situation.

So I decided to do it again. I got more eggs and this time about 7 out of 12 hatched. But one chick died of early age due to an illness called wry neck. Nothing in my end could be done. I gave a goodbye as we put him next to our 6 chicks in our backyard.

As I raised the remaining 6 they grew so fast. Traited with feathers by week two, growing faster than ever. And as they grew into the tote, another disaster happened. Two of them died because I left them outside and they died of low ventilation. I was determined to raise the other 4 well. After week 3 they didn’t even need a brooder, their feathers could outdo the cold and the heat. I gave them a bigger waterer and food and also gave them a wood shaving flooring with pine. But one occurring issue I found was that they spilled a lot of their food and were picky eaters. But I dealt with that by placing a wood bar below the waterer so the wood would absorb it and not the shavings. By the time I left for summer camp they layed their first eggs. I learned a week before there was one male and 3 females, a perfect ratio. I also realized when I came back from camp they were fertilized, as the male was climbing on the girls backs to mate with them.

I collected a lot of eggs but unfortunately recently they died because of the cold. I prepared them for summer but not for winter and didn’t last long since I haven’t fed them for 2 days. The male escaped to and was found dead in a random corner. I was shocked on that day and didn’t know what to do. But I wanted to keep going and had button quails the same time they were there and that led me to not care for them. And yet as a curse the young button quails died too, three of them out of 12, a horrible hatch rate and died because of pasty butt, accidently smushed by the holes in which it couldn’t escape and died smushed, and the last one, although it went through a week and a half it also died of lonliness.
